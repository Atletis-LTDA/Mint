Atletis gives you a live view of your event's financial performance from the moment the first registration comes in. You don't need to wait until the event closes to understand how sales are going — the dashboard updates in real time as participants register and payments are confirmed. After the event, a post-event financial report summarizes total revenue, platform fees, and the net amount transferred to you. This page explains what data is available, how to read it, and how to use it to strengthen future editions of your event.

Open your organizer dashboard, navigate to your event, and select **Reports** to access these metrics at any time. The data reflects the current state of your registrations and does not require a manual refresh.

## Monitoring payment status

The reports section breaks registrations down by payment status so you can track which participants have completed their purchase and which are still pending.

| Payment status | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Paid** | Payment confirmed — participant is fully registered | No action needed; participant will appear in the check-in list |
| **Pending** | Payment not yet confirmed — common with boleto (*bank slip*) | Monitor over the following business days; boleto can take up to 3 days to clear |
| **Failed** | Payment was declined or abandoned | Registration is incomplete; the spot may still be available depending on your capacity settings |

Tracking pending payments in the days before your event helps you anticipate your final headcount more accurately and flag participants who may arrive expecting entry before their boleto has cleared.

## Post-event financial report

After your event closes, Atletis generates a post-event financial report that includes:

- **Total gross revenue** — the sum of all confirmed paid registrations
- **Platform fee breakdown** — the Atletis service fee applied to each transaction
- **Net payout amount** — the amount transferred to your bank account after fees

The report gives you a single document to hand to your accountant or to use for budget reconciliation. You can export it directly from the **Reports** section of your dashboard.

<Note>
  **Payout timing:** your net payout is scheduled for the next business day after the event date. Make sure your banking details are correctly configured in your Atletis account settings before the event so there are no delays in receiving your funds.
</Note>

## Exporting reports for accounting

To export any report from the dashboard:

1. Navigate to your event in the organizer dashboard.
2. Open the **Reports** section.
3. Select the report you want — sales summary, payment status breakdown, or post-event financial report.
4. Click **Export** to download the file.

Exported reports include all the rows visible in the current filtered view. If you want a full report, clear any active filters before exporting. If you want a report scoped to a specific category or batch, apply the relevant filter first.

## Using report data to improve future events

The sales data your event generates is one of the most useful inputs for planning the next edition. After the event, spend time reviewing:

**Which batch sold the most registrations?** If your first batch sold out in two days but your third batch still had spots available two weeks before the event, you likely priced the early batches too conservatively. Consider opening a larger early-bird allocation next time.

**Which categories were most popular?** If one category accounted for 60% of registrations, that category should drive your infrastructure planning — number of start waves, kit volume per size, number of check-in stations, and finish line flow. Categories with low uptake may be worth merging or re-pricing for the next edition.

**What was the revenue-per-registration trend across batches?** Rising average revenue per registration in later batches can indicate strong demand and price sensitivity. If participants consistently paid the highest price available, you may have room to increase base pricing in future editions.

**What was the no-show rate?** The attendance data captured by the check-in app, compared against the total paid registrations in your report, gives you a no-show percentage. A high no-show rate may signal that participants are registering speculatively or that your communication before the event day needs reinforcement.

These insights cost nothing extra — they come from the data your registrations already generate. Reviewing them systematically after each event is one of the fastest ways to improve the next one.
