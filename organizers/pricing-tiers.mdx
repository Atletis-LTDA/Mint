Pricing tiers — called **lotes** in Brazilian sports events — let you sell registrations in sequential phases, each with its own price and availability window. Rather than charging one flat fee from the moment you open registrations to the day of the event, you reward early believers with a lower price and gradually increase it as the event date approaches. The result is a steady cash flow from the start, a built-in sense of urgency, and a participant list that grows organically throughout your campaign.

## Why pricing tiers work

A lote is more than a row in a pricing table. Each tier serves a distinct commercial purpose:

- **Rewards commitment** — participants who register early take a risk on your event and deserve to pay less.
- **Generates early revenue** — cash from the first lote helps you cover initial logistics costs before the event date.
- **Creates real urgency** — a clearly communicated deadline or slot limit gives fence-sitters a genuine reason to decide now.
- **Spreads demand** — distributing sales across multiple phases smooths your workload and your marketing calendar.
- **Provides performance signals** — watching how quickly each lote fills tells you whether your pricing and promotion are working, while there is still time to adjust.

<Note>
  A lote transition that happens without warning feels like a trick to participants. Always announce the next lote's price and trigger upfront — on the registration page, in your social posts, and in reminder messages before the deadline hits.
</Note>

## Planning your lote structure

Before opening registrations, decide how many lotes make sense for your event. A small local race may need only two or three; a large multi-category championship may warrant four or more. For each lote, define four things: its **objective**, its **price**, its **transition trigger**, and its **communication plan**.

| Lote | Objective | Suggested trigger | Communication |
|------|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| Pre-sale / Lote Zero | Test demand, generate initial cash | Short date window or a small fixed quantity | Exclusive announcement to your mailing list and close partners |
| Lote 1 | Reward early registrants | Fixed date or first N slots sold | Broad launch campaign with clear deadline |
| Lote 2 | Maintain sales momentum | Date or quantity milestone | Reminder message a few days before transition |
| Lote 3 (final) | Capture last-minute decisions | Closing date before the event | Countdown and final-call messaging |

<Tip>
  Start with a price that makes sense for your costs — then build upward. If Lote 1 is already at your maximum sustainable price, you have nowhere to go and no incentive to create urgency. Work backward from your break-even number to set each tier.
</Tip>

## Practical example: a road race with three lotes

The table below shows how three lotes might be structured for a 10 km road race with a single adult category.

| | Lote 1 | Lote 2 | Lote 3 |
|-|--------|--------|--------|
| **Price** | R$ 60 | R$ 80 | R$ 100 |
| **Slots available** | 100 | 150 | Remaining |
| **Transition trigger** | 100 slots sold OR 45 days before event | 150 slots sold OR 20 days before event | Closes 48 h before event |
| **Objective** | Reward early commitment | Sustain momentum | Capture last-minute sign-ups |

Participants who register in Lote 1 pay R$ 40 less than those who wait until the last lote — a clear, tangible reward for acting early.

## Setting up lotes on Atletis

<Steps>
  <Step title="Log in and open your event">
    Go to [app.atletis.com.br/meus-eventos](https://app.atletis.com.br/meus-eventos) and click on the event you want to configure. If you haven't created the event yet, click **Criar Evento** on the Atletis home page and fill in the event details first (sport type, date, location, organizer contact).
  </Step>

  <Step title="Navigate to the registration and pricing section">
    In the event editor, locate the section for **Ingressos / Inscrições** (registrations). This is where you define all lotes for your event.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Create your first lote">
    Click **Adicionar lote** (Add tier). Fill in:
    - **Name** — e.g. "Lote 1 – Early Bird"
    - **Price** — the registration fee for this tier
    - **Available slots** — leave blank for unlimited, or enter a number to cap this tier
    - **Start date / time** — when this lote opens
    - **End date / time** — when this lote closes (the automatic transition trigger)
  </Step>

  <Step title="Configure the automatic transition trigger">
    Atletis supports two transition triggers — you can use either or combine both:

    - **By date** — the lote closes automatically at the date and time you set, and the next lote opens immediately.
    - **By quantity** — the lote closes as soon as the slot limit is reached, even if the end date hasn't arrived yet.

    You do not need to manually switch lotes. Once a trigger fires, the platform moves to the next active lote on its own.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Add subsequent lotes">
    Repeat Step 3 for each additional lote (Lote 2, Lote 3, etc.), increasing the price each time. Make sure the start date of each new lote aligns with the end date of the previous one so there is no gap where registrations are unavailable.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Review and publish">
    Review all lotes — prices, slot limits, and transition dates — before publishing the event. Once registrations are live, participants can see the current lote price on your event page. When a lote closes, the page automatically displays the next lote's price and availability.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What participants see

During checkout, participants always see the **currently active lote** and its price. They do not need to select a lote manually — the platform determines which tier is open based on date and remaining slots. If you communicate upcoming lote transitions transparently, participants will understand the price they see is time-sensitive, which encourages faster decisions.

## Best practices

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="Announce all tiers upfront" icon="megaphone">
    Publish the full lote schedule — prices, dates, and slot limits — on your event page before you open sales. Transparency builds trust and helps participants plan.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Use both triggers together" icon="sliders">
    Setting both a date limit and a slot limit means the lote closes whichever comes first. This protects you if sales move faster than expected.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Avoid too many tiers" icon="list-check">
    More than four or five lotes can dilute urgency. Each tier needs a clear reason to exist. If two lotes have the same objective, merge them.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Align lotes with your financial plan" icon="calculator">
    Price each lote based on your actual event costs and revenue goal, not on what feels like a round number. Knowing your break-even point prevents under-pricing your early lotes.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
  Never change the transition date of an active lote to a later date without communicating the change to participants who have already registered. Surprise extensions can feel dishonest and undermine confidence in future editions of your event.
</Warning>
